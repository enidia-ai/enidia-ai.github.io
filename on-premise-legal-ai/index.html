On-premise legal AI

On-premise legal AI: document review that runs on hardware you own

The work stays in the building. So does the bill.

On-premise legal AI runs the model on a machine inside your firm's network instead of in a vendor's cloud. Documents are read where they already sit, so nothing is sent to a third-party model and there is no transfer to assess. Enidia's AssociAte works this way in production: one workstation of about €5,000, a licence, and no metered usage on top.

Deploy. Where the work happens, and what never leaves the building.

What changes

Who can see your documents, and what a heavy month costs

On-premise is the part of this that competitors cannot copy without rebuilding their business, and it changes two things at once: who can see your documents, and what a heavy month costs you.

Privilege stays intact

Nothing is sent to a third-party model. There is no processor to add to your record, no sub-processor list to chase, and no transfer to assess, because there is no transfer.

The documents you cannot send anywhere

Matters under embargo, client data with contractual bans on external processing, regulated material. These are exactly the documents with the most manual work in them, and the only ones a cloud tool can never touch.

Fixed cost, unlimited runs

The machine costs what it costs. Running a fifty-question form over three hundred documents costs the same as running it over three, so nobody has to ask permission before using it.

A machine, not a data centre

It runs perfectly well on hardware costing around €5,000. That is one workstation under a desk, which puts it within reach of a three-partner boutique and a single fund administrator, not only of firms with an infrastructure budget.

Scaling costs about €5,000

Double the people using it and you have not doubled a bill, because there is no per-seat bill. If the queue gets long, you add a second machine. That is the whole upgrade path, and you own what you bought.

It still works when the vendor changes

Price rises, model deprecations, an outage, an acquisition. Software on your own hardware keeps doing the same thing tomorrow as it did today.

Your corpus is searchable

Because the system lives where your documents live, it can work across your own back catalogue, the firm's own precedents, not just the file in front of you.

An artefact you can hand over

Every run produces a certificate: each element, its state, its verbatim source and its locator. It goes into the matter file, to the client, or in front of a regulator.

How you get there

Try it in our cloud first. Bring it inside when it has earned it.

Test the tool, in our cloud

Be clear about what this step is: the trial runs on our hosted environment, not on your premises. It exists to test whether the tool does the task, not to do the work. So bring your own checklist and a document you are free to share, a public prospectus or an old matter that has been anonymised. Confidential client material belongs on the machine, and only there.

Bring it inside

Once it has proved itself on your work, you buy the hardware it runs on and licence the tool. It moves onto your premises, your documents stop travelling, and the running cost stops depending on how much you use it.
