For law firms

Legal AI for law firms in Luxembourg: closing checklists and due diligence lists filled from the documents

Give it the work you'd never give AI.

Much of a Luxembourg law firm's document work is a fixed list: a closing or condition-precedent checklist, a due diligence request list, a playbook review. Filling it means finding each answer in the agreements, tying it to the clause it came from, and saying clearly what is not there. Enidia's AssociAte does that work from the transaction documents, on a machine inside the firm.

A closing checklist against a partnership agreement. One checklist, one LPA, one pass, and every answer traceable to the clause it came from.

The problem

Friday afternoon. "Fill this in by Monday."

A partner hands an associate a fund's LPA and the firm's checklist. The LPA runs well over a hundred pages. The checklist asks the usual questions: the management fee, the key person clause, transfers, most favoured nation.

Option one: read it all

The associate's weekend goes into the LPA. For a large one, that is days of work, and the next fund brings the same checklist.

Option two: ask an AI, then check it anyway

A general assistant fills the checklist in minutes. But nobody hands the partner answers they have not checked, so the associate goes back to the LPA to confirm each one. By the last answer, the work has taken almost as long as reading the LPA in the first place.

Monday morning, question 1

Where is it written?

Question 2

What did the AI guess?

Question 3

What did it miss?

Neither option gives a quick answer. One costs days of reading, the other days of checking. Replace the LPA with a data room, a prospectus or a company's articles and contracts: same process, same problem.

All four workflows

The partner's test

"Where did you take this from?"

A partner delegates the reading to a junior associate and checks the result. The check is quick for one reason: the partner can always ask where it came from, and the associate opens the page. Enidia answers the same way, with the page and the quoted passage behind every answer. The check is reading the answer next to its quote.

Proven

The answer and the passage that proves it. Click, and the agreement opens on that passage. The check is reading one quote, not re-reading the clause.

Uncertain

The tool checked its own answer against the passage and the checklist's requirement and found a problem. It says so and hands the field to the lawyer.

Not found

A requirement the documents do not answer. It is reported as missing, with the reason, never filled with a guess. For a closing, the missing item is the finding that matters most.

What on-premise means

Knowledge transfer

It learns from your lawyers, and what it learns stays in the firm

A firm's knowledge sits in the checklists it writes, in reviewers' comments and in the checklists it has already completed. Enidia takes that knowledge in four ways and uses it on the next run.

0 · Import

Give it your checklist

Import the checklist as the firm uses it. Its explanations, sample answers and reviewers' notes become each field's starting definition, which a senior reviews before the first run.

1 · Comment

Comment on a field

While reviewing, the associate comments on an answer, where the work is done. A senior approves the comment and it goes into the field's definition. The next run uses it. No comment changes the system on its own.

2 · Correct

Hand it a solved checklist

Export the draft, correct it in Word, upload the corrected version. The tool compares every field, its answer against the lawyer's, and writes the commentary that closes the gap.

3 · Archive

Batch training on your archive

Upload the checklists the firm has already filled in, with their documents, and run it overnight. Years of completed work become the template's knowledge in one night; an editor then approves what the tool learned.

What it learns is plain-language field definitions a senior can read, edit and approve, stored on the firm's server and never used to train a shared model. A partner's approved comment shapes the template every associate runs.

Questions

For law firms, in plain answers

Does it work on an LPA or a closing checklist?

Yes. One checklist, one LPA, one pass, and every answer traceable to the clause it came from. Closing and condition-precedent lists come back with each requirement proven, uncertain or not found, and every missing item reported with the reason.

How long does a 25-hour checklist review take with it?

In our own timed run, an LPA checklist that took 25 hours by hand was completed in 4. It is our own figure, not a client's, and no firm has verified it.

Do client documents leave the firm?

Not in production. The tool runs on a machine inside your firm, nothing is sent to a third-party model, and privilege stays intact.

Can we test it on a real matter?

Test it first on a document you are free to share: a public document or an old matter that has been anonymised. The trial runs in our cloud. Confidential client material belongs on the machine in your firm, and only there.

How is checking its answers faster than doing the work?

Every answer opens on the passage that proves it, so the check is reading one quote, not re-reading the agreement. Fields the tool itself marks uncertain or not found are where the lawyer's attention goes first.

Does it learn the firm's way of answering?

Yes, in four ways: the imported checklist's own notes, comments approved by a senior, a corrected Word draft, and batch training on the firm's completed checklists. What it learns stays on the firm's server.

Is it a general legal assistant?

No. It does one task: a fixed list, filled from documents, to a standard somebody can defend. If you want one tool for everything, a general assistant is the better buy.
