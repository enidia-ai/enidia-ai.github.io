DORA Article 30 contract review: check every supplier contract against the required clauses

DORA Article 30 sets out the clauses a contract with an ICT third-party service provider has to contain. Reviewing a supplier portfolio means checking each contract against that list, one contract at a time, and the missing clauses are the deliverable. Enidia's AssociAte runs the list against each contract, ties every finding to its clause, and reports every gap rather than guessing.

Questions

Contract review, in plain answers

What does a DORA Article 30 review check?

Each contract with an ICT supplier against the clauses the regulation requires, one contract at a time. The output shows which clauses are there, with their wording and location, and which are missing.

What happens when a contract is missing a required clause?

It is reported as absent: a finding you sign off, never a blank and never something plausible filled in to cover it.

Can the same review run against NIS2 or our own playbook?

Yes. Any defined set of legal requirements works the same way: DORA, NIS2, an internal playbook or a processor agreement standard.

Does a correction on one contract carry to the next?

Yes. A correction taught on the first contract is already there when the second one runs.

Where are the contracts processed?

On a machine inside your network. Nothing is sent to a third-party model, so there is no processor to add to your record and no transfer to assess.
