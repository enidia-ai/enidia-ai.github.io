For small law firms

Legal AI for a small law firm: one computer, from three lawyers

Give it the work you'd never give AI.

Most legal AI is sold per named seat with usage metered on top, which makes it expensive for a firm of a few lawyers and turns every large job into a budget question. A small firm does not need that model. Enidia is €125 a lawyer a month from three lawyers, on a computer from about €4,000 under a desk, with nothing metered underneath, so the partner who buys it can use it on every checklist without rationing it.

What a small firm buys

One machine, and nobody has to ration it

A machine, not a data centre

A MacBook Pro with enough memory, about €4,000, carries a small team on one machine; a server for the firm costs €6,400 to €8,700. One box under a desk, within reach of a three-partner boutique and a single fund administrator, not only of firms with an infrastructure budget.

Fixed cost, unlimited runs

The machine costs what it costs. Running a fifty-question form over three hundred documents costs the same as running it over three, so nobody has to ask permission before using it.

Scaling costs a licence, not a meter

Add a lawyer and you add €125 a month, never a bill for the pages they run. If the queue gets long, you add a second computer. That is the whole upgrade path, and you own what you bought.

Who this is built for

The largest firms build their own AI. Everyone else needs something to buy.

The firms with hundreds of technology staff build their own tools on their own servers. A firm of three, five or twelve lawyers cannot, and the leading cloud tools are sold in seat packs it will never fill. Enidia is built for the firm in between: a product it can own, not a project it has to staff.

“Outside the top 50 firms, the build-versus-buy selection or strategy is not applicable to anyone.”

Alex Finkel, Offit Kurman, 2026. Those firms cannot build. Enidia is for them: one machine the firm owns, with the verified workflow already built and tested once, for every firm.

A new questionnaire in an afternoon, not a project

Import the form, run it on one document, correct the first answers. The template is ready for the next matter. No integration work, no consultant, and nobody in the firm has to become the AI person.

Word in, Word out

The checklist goes in as the Word file you already use and comes back as the filled form, ready to sign and send. One review screen shows each answer next to its quoted passage. There is nothing else to learn.

The firm's knowledge stays when an associate leaves

Every correction and every comment a reviewer approves goes into the template, on the firm's own machine. When the person who wrote it moves on, the method stays, readable by whoever runs the next checklist.

Questions

For small firms, in plain answers

Is there a minimum number of seats?

Three lawyers. The licence is €125 a lawyer a month, so a firm of three pays €375 a month plus a computer, bought once. Nothing is metered on top.

What hardware does a small firm need?

A MacBook Pro with enough memory, about €4,000, is enough for a small team on one machine; a server costs €6,400 to €8,700. That puts it within reach of a three-partner boutique, not only of firms with an infrastructure budget.

What does a heavy month cost?

The same as a light one. Volume changes how long a run takes, not what it costs.

Can we try it before buying anything?

Yes. The trial runs in our cloud on your own checklist and a document you are free to share. Nothing is installed and nothing is signed to find out whether it works.

Do client documents leave the firm?

Not in production. The tool runs on the machine in your office, and nothing is sent to a third-party model.

How much setting up does it need?

An afternoon for a new questionnaire. You import the form from Word, run it on one document and correct the first answers; the template is then ready for the next matter. There is no integration project and no new system to learn, because the checklist goes in as Word and comes out as Word.

What happens to what the tool has learned if a lawyer leaves?

It stays. Approved corrections and comments live in the template on the firm's own machine, in plain language anyone can read, so the method does not walk out with the person who taught it.
