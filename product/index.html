Verification

Three statuses, and you can tell them apart

Each field gets one of three statuses. The lawyer sees at a glance where to spend attention, and the proof for a green row is one click away.

Proven

The answer and the passage that proves it. Click, and the document opens on that passage. The check is reading one quote, not re-reading the document.

Uncertain

The system checked its own answer against the passage and the field's requirements and found a problem. It says so and hands the field to the lawyer, instead of hiding the doubt in fluent prose.

Not found

A required item the documents do not answer. It is reported as missing, with the reason, and never filled with a guess. For a regulatory filing, a missing item is the finding that matters most.

No evidence, no answer. A field with no passage found in the documents gets no answer, only not found. The lawyer starts with the orange and the grey; green rows need only the one-click check.

What comes out: the filled form with its evidence

Every run returns the filled form with, for each element, its status, its verbatim source and its locator. You review it, correct it and sign it; then it goes into the matter file, to the client, or in front of a regulator. The workflow is the same every time: the list arrives, it gets filled, you check it, you argue with it, you correct it, and you sign what goes out.

Transparency

What is missing is reported, with the reason.

Most tools show you what they found. The AssociAte also shows what it did not find and why, and which searched pages gave no evidence, so the reviewer sees the gaps as clearly as the answers.

Not found, with the reason

Every item without an answer is marked in place, with why: not present in the document, not yet extracted, or evidence that could not be confirmed. Nothing is left silently blank.

Pages searched, pages used

For each item, the tool shows which pages it searched, out of the document's total, and which of them gave evidence, instead of leaving the reviewer to trust a model's silence.

Dropped answers

Answers the tool generated but could not tie to a passage word for word are discarded. The reviewer sees "unconfirmed evidence" and the reason, instead of a confident guess.

The text, or the page itself. Each source shows the text the tool read or an image of the original page. Hidden text, such as invisible instructions aimed at AI, shows up when the two differ.

The workflow

A fixed workflow, not a chat that improvises.

A general assistant decides each time how to do the task, so two runs give two different results. A questionnaire here runs as a fixed pipeline with verification built in: the same steps on every item, every document, every year.

1

Your questionnaire, from Word

The regulator's form or your own checklist, imported once. It becomes a template you reuse on every later document.

2

Your documents

The prospectus, the agreements, the fund's file. English, French or German, one document or a set. They stay on your computer.

3

Answer and verify, per item

For each field: find the passages, answer, then check the answer against its passage. The same steps for every item and every document.

4

Status and flags

Proven, uncertain or not found. Possible omissions and the pages the tool did not use are flagged, so the reviewer sees what is missing too.

5

The lawyer signs

Reviews every answer with its evidence one click away, corrects where needed, exports the filled form to Word and signs. Corrections are kept with the template.

Why it works on small models

Narrow questions, evidence in front

The pipeline asks the model one narrow question at a time with the passages in front of it. That is what lets open models on one server in the firm answer reliably.

Repeatable, so the same questionnaire gives the same kind of result on every fund and every year. Defensible, so the work can be shown to a partner, an auditor or the regulator, field by field.

Knowledge transfer

It learns from your lawyers, and what it learns stays in the firm.

A firm's knowledge sits in the checklists it writes, in reviewers' comments and in the checklists it has already completed. The AssociAte takes that knowledge in four ways and uses it on the next run.

0

Give it your checklist

Import the checklist as the firm uses it. Its explanations, sample answers and reviewers' notes become each field's starting definition, which a senior reviews before the first run.

1

Comment on a field

While reviewing, the associate comments on an answer, where the work is done. A senior approves the comment and it goes into the field's definition. No comment changes the system on its own.

2

Hand it a solved checklist

Export the draft, correct it in Word, upload the corrected version and click improve. The tool compares every field against the lawyer's answer and writes the commentary that closes the gap.

3

Batch training on your archive

Upload the checklists the firm has already filled in, with their documents, and run it overnight. Years of completed work become the template's knowledge; an editor then approves what was learned.

Where the knowledge lives

On the firm's server: definitions, comments and completed checklists stay inside the firm's network and serve the firm alone. What it learns is plain-language field definitions a senior can read, edit and approve, not a black box. Nothing the firm teaches it reaches another firm or a vendor, and it is never used to train a shared model. A partner's approved comment shapes the template every associate runs.

Questions

The AssociAte, in plain answers

What does the AssociAte do with a checklist?

It takes your checklist, form, survey or template and fills it from the documents. Every answer carries its page and its exact words.

What happens when the documents do not answer a question?

The item comes back marked not found, with the reason: not present in the documents, not yet extracted, or evidence that could not be confirmed. Nothing is filled with a guess, and nothing is left silently blank.

How do I check an answer?

In one click. A proven answer opens on the passage that proves it, so the check is reading one quote next to the answer rather than re-reading the document. For each item you also see which pages were searched and which gave evidence.

Does it learn our vocabulary and corrections?

Yes, in four ways: the explanations in your checklist become each field's starting definition, a reviewer's comment on a field goes into the definition once a senior approves it, a corrected Word draft can be uploaded so the tool closes the gap itself, and the checklists your firm has already completed can be run as batch training overnight. What it learns is plain-language field definitions that stay on your server.

What does it show about what it did not find?

Every item without an answer is marked in place with the reason. For each item you see which pages were searched and which gave evidence. An answer the tool generated but could not tie to a passage word for word is discarded and shown as unconfirmed evidence, never as a confident guess.

Do our lawyers have to learn a new tool?

Very little. The checklist goes in as a Word file and comes out as a filled Word form, ready to sign. In between there is one review screen: each answer, its status and its quoted passage side by side. Setting up a new questionnaire takes an afternoon.

Can we try it without installing anything?

Yes. The trial runs in our cloud on your own checklist and a document you are free to share. Nothing you send is used to train anything, and it is deleted when the trial ends.
