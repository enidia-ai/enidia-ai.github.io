What law firms need to know about AI

Adam Kaczmarczyk, LL.M., Product Lead, Enidia AI · Questions by Tomer Libal, CEO
November 8, 2026

I asked our Product Lead, Adam Kaczmarczyk, three questions about how law firms should approach AI.

Adam holds an LL.M. and has also studied data science. People keep asking him to choose between law and technology. He refuses.

Here is what he told me.

What is the most important message for law firms adopting AI?

“Firms need to adapt and adopt the best available solutions, with efficiency as a priority. But they must also meet strict confidentiality requirements and ensure that results can be verified. Independence matters, too. As AI becomes central to more workflows, reliance on an external model provider can leave a firm vulnerable. That is why locally deployed solutions are attracting increasing attention.”

You chose to study both data science and law. Why?

“I have always been interested in both the humanities and science, and being young is an ideal time to develop a range of skills. The two fields also have much in common: logic, analytical thinking, and interpreting evidence. Today, data science can help answer complex legal questions by structuring knowledge and analysing data.”

What is the biggest challenge for law firms today?

“In short: the pace of change. Work that used to take weeks can now be completed in days or even hours. To keep up, firms need to integrate AI thoughtfully, with proper planning and analysis, rather than adopting whichever tool happens to be popular. They must also consider compliance, including data processing obligations and liability for their systems’ outputs.”
