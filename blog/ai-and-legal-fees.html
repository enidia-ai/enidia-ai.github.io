Billing for AI time: what the Tenth Circuit and the New York bar said

The Enidia AI team
November 5, 2026

On 4 November 2026 a US appeals court upheld a 115,000 dollar legal fee award even though the lawyers had used AI. A day earlier, the New York State Bar Association set out how AI costs may be charged to clients. Together they show that AI does not shrink a bill automatically, because the lawyer still has to check the work, and checking is work.

What did the Tenth Circuit decide about AI and fee awards?

The ABA Journal reported on 4 November 2026, from Reuters, that the 10th US Circuit Court of Appeals upheld a 115,000 dollar fee award to defence lawyers at Brownstein Hyatt Farber Schreck, in a trade secrets dispute brought by Flux Drive.

The losing side argued the bill should have been smaller because the defence had used AI. A unanimous three judge panel disagreed. Judge Carolyn McHugh wrote that “Plaintiffs have not shown that defense counsel’s use of AI rendered the hours that they actually expended unreasonable.” The judge accepted that AI tools can make lawyers more efficient, while stressing that lawyers must still verify the authorities and arguments themselves. The defence said it had checked every piece of AI assisted research.

What did the New York State Bar Association say about billing for AI?

On 3 November 2026 the ABA Journal reported, from Law360, that the association had adopted a report from its Committee on Artificial Intelligence and Emerging Technologies. It treats the cost of AI software as overhead, chargeable to a client as a disbursement only when tied to a specific matter and disclosed. Lawyers generally need not disclose AI used for administrative work such as spell checking or calendaring, provided it does not affect fees.

The headline of that report says lawyers should not bill for time saved by AI. The substance, as described, is narrower. We have read the press account, not the report itself.

Do the two conflict?

No. Both rest on the same fact: a lawyer who signs still has to check, and checking is work. That is why the fee award survived. The court did not ask how fast the research was produced, but whether the hours spent were reasonable.

It is also why the question stays open. Verification is invisible. A reviewed answer and an unreviewed answer look identical on the page. The defence in Denver won partly because it could say exactly what it had checked, and the other side could not point at a single entry.

Does this matter outside the United States?

The rulings do not apply here, but the pressure does. The EU AI Act is in force, and the CSSF’s 2026 annual report named hallucination and loss of human control among the risks facing the financial sector, with a survey of supervised firms on their use of AI. “Our people check everything” will be worth as much as the evidence behind it.

Questions

Can a court cut legal fees because a firm used AI?

Not automatically. On 4 November 2026 the 10th US Circuit upheld a 115,000 dollar award, finding the challenger had not shown the hours were unreasonable.

Can a firm charge clients for AI software?

Under the New York report, it is overhead, and may be passed on only when tied to a matter and disclosed.

Does AI remove the duty to check?

No. Both point the same way: the lawyer who signs remains responsible.
